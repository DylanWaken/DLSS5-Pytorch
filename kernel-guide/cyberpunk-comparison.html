Measured speed: separate the three timing scopes

Device: NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Workstation Edition. CUDA events bracket each eager launch sequence and synchronize its end; exposed host enqueue gaps can be included. These are not captured CUDA-graph replay timings. CPU wall time uses host timing across explicit synchronization. Records, plans, buffers, weights and validation are prepared outside the timed intervals; no NCU run overlaps these measurements. Graph-capture correctness is a separate acceptance check.

  1. RGB trunk: the prepared PLANE16-output sequence on a precomputed pooled input, including the final phase-3 C32 output view.
  2. Full GPU: preprocess → RGB trunk → post, including texture/surface device-to-device copies. Host upload/download, decode and encode are excluded.
  3. CPU wall: includes upload, full GPU, synchronization and RGB download. Still decode is excluded; video PNG decode/convert is included. Encoding and output-file writes are excluded in both.

Only these three scopes were timed independently. Do not subtract the CPU wall time from a GPU scope to claim a separately measured host-time percentage or a causal host bottleneck.

Preprocess, postprocess and device-to-device copies are timed in the full-GPU and CPU-wall scopes but stay outside this trunk census. These are schedule facts, not timings, and they do not predict the measured ratios.

What was reused, and what changed?

Most operators reuse the existing CUDA math. Small31 adds its own weight loader, phase/role schedule and native host plan. Its eight global-attention calls select two new precision-specific kernels with FP32 persistent P×V accumulation and full FP32 denominator reduction. The original 81 kernels and legacy plans remain supported.

Both RGB paths reuse the existing phase-3 C32 output-view kernel for their final descriptor. That writes the PLANE16 layout expected by postprocessing without an extra repack call. The base plan’s TILE4 contract remains separate. Small has 109 logical trunk calls (76 compute/repack +33 clear), versus Legacy71’s 185 (152 +33); at 4K FP16 those become 117 and 193 physical split launches. The counts do not predict the speed ratio, and pre/post work is outside them.

Read the model and runtime design, new FP16 attention lesson, and new FP8 attention lesson. The newer DLL also carries unchanged HT weights; this page compares Legacy71 with the separately trained Small31 resource, not two different HT models.
